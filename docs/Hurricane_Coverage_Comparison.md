# Hurricane coverage comparison

Historical twelve-storm screen. The active research sample is Ian, Milton, Idalia
and Beryl; current rebuilt counts and source-review qualifications are in
[the research briefing](Hurricane_Repricing_Summary.html). The table below is retained
as the earlier screening snapshot and can differ from refreshed sibling inputs.

This targeted screen compares TRACE and archive coverage for eleven hurricane landfalls in Florida or the US Gulf/Atlantic during 2016–2024, plus Texas landfall Beryl. It retains Ian and Milton. Selection was based on landfall geography and usable TRACE coverage, not bond-price changes. Debby is included as a competing event and was a tropical-storm landfall. This is not an exhaustive landfall census.

All storms use inclusive landfall-minus-45 through landfall-plus-60 calendar-day windows. Eligible CUSIPs have one strict high-confidence bridge match and no title-propagated evidence. Trades are grouped by CUSIP before bridge attribution. Exposure categories are relevant-region hurricane exposure, mapped no-hurricane exposure, other hurricane region, and unknown/ambiguous; the last two are not treated as unexposed. Relevant-region counts use Florida regions for Florida storms and US/Gulf tags elsewhere, so the latter is a broad exposure screen. The market's exposure geography is not precise enough to establish an individual state for every deal.

A customer trade is TRACE `contra_party=C`; `dealer_side=S` means the customer bought and `B` means the customer sold. Balanced trading requires at least three customer trades strictly before and strictly after landfall; day zero is separate. The eight-day event interval is landfall minus four through plus three, inclusive. Density is observed customer CUSIP-days divided by eligible CUSIPs × 8. Repeated means customer trading on at least two distinct event-interval dates. No synthetic days are created. CUSIPs are distinct from bridge pairs and deals.

Class support is a parsed N-PORT title class matched uniquely to the issuance tranche table. It is not manual verification of security class and exposure together; verified pair count is zero. Missing/conflicting class remains unknown. No tranche EL or attachment probability is inferred from unsupported mappings.

Prediction contracts were searched in the local read-only SQLite archive across IDs, event/group metadata, nested descriptions and rules. A storm-specific contract must resolve on that named storm's landfall/intensity. Geographic seasonal city wind thresholds and broad multi-storm thresholds remain proxies. A usable contract needs actual non-null Yes-outcome observations, and trade overlap requires an observed probability date equal to an actual eligible CUSIP trade date. Open/close span alone does not qualify. Polymarket price-history points are token prices (not trades); timestamps map to Eastern dates. Kalshi observations are daily Yes candle closes, assigned to the preceding Eastern calendar date; they are not executions. Date overlap does not establish intraday ordering.

Event-8 density is rounded. “History” in the contract column counts contracts with one or more non-null Yes observations within the storm's full -45/+60 window. Contract metadata and exact question/rules, platform IDs, opening/closing dates, outcome identity, observation counts and overlap details are in `data/derived/comparison_contracts.csv`. Milton's 19 seasonal proxies include Tampa, Orlando and Miami city wind thresholds; they do not estimate Milton's landfall probability. No local archive contract is classified as explicitly Milton-specific. The separate broad Cat 5 before-election market is a multi-storm proxy, not a Milton-specific outcome. Milton has zero explicit-contract CUSIP trade overlap and 72 eligible CUSIPs overlapping seasonal proxy price dates. For Beryl, the two storm-specific Polymarket contracts are “Will Beryl hit US as a hurricane?” and “Will Beryl hit the US as a tropical storm?” Their Yes histories each have 123 non-null event-interval observations; 23 eligible CUSIPs overlap at least one contract observation date, while two have repeat overlap in the eight-day interval. Those outcomes are conterminous-US landfall status, not Texas loss severity. For Idalia, two storm-specific intensity/landfall markets have usable histories, but only nine eligible CUSIPs overlap any observation and none repeatedly overlaps within the event interval. The saved inventory lists six non-null event-interval dates per Beryl contract due Eastern-date conversion; the dates extend through July 9, the day after the July 8 landfall.

The local SQLite archive contains no explicit Milton metadata match across contract IDs and nested event/market metadata. That establishes only absence from this local archive, not that a market never existed. No NHC advisory or wind-probability files were found in the project, sibling repositories, or Downloads inventory; they were not confirmed unavailable. Public official timestamped archive pages are indexed for [Beryl](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/archive/2024/al02/), [Idalia](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/archive/2023/al10/), [Ian](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/archive/2022/al09/), [Helene](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/archive/2024/al09/), and [Milton](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/archive/2024/al14/). The [NHC product description](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/aboutnhcgraphics.shtml) and [GIS wind-probability archive](https://www.nhc.noaa.gov/gis/archive_wsp.php) describe obtainable products. Per-advisory cadence, timestamps, location points, units, and cumulative horizons still need targeted inventory and QA. No product is confirmed unavailable.

**Recommendation.** Beryl currently has the strongest local expectations-market coverage: two actual storm-specific Yes price histories and the most CUSIP trade-date overlap, with 23 CUSIPs meeting any-date overlap but only two repeatedly overlapping during the event interval. It is a US landfall outcome and Texas exposure is broad, so a closer audit should verify geography and matched-tranche coverage before changing the case. Idalia is the strongest Florida-specific probability alternative, but its trade-date overlap is sparse. Milton remains suitable for descriptive repricing and possible recovery, but the local archive supports season-long city-strike proxies rather than Milton probability; a probability-and-repricing design needs archived NHC forecast/wind-probability snapshots or new targeted market data. Ian remains suitable for the concentrated-loss study: its window overlaps Nicole and only seven eligible CUSIPs have balanced trade counts. Ida deserves a closer audit because it has 127 eligible CUSIPs, 51 relevant-region-exposed, 44 title-supported class plus relevant exposure, and seven balanced CUSIPs—more bond coverage than Ian. The separate issuance loss table has direct high-confidence total-loss entries across three Ida deals and three Ian deals; these are source loss labels, not conclusions from bond prices. Do not automatically replace either case.

Coverage counts measure linked opportunity, not repricing or causal effects. Overlapping windows, the other listed storms, and unlisted concurrent events complicate attribution. Best next acquisition is a small, validated NHC advisory/WSP snapshot panel for Beryl, Idalia, Milton, Ian and Ida, including release times and location-specific cumulative horizons. Then manually verify CUSIP-to-tranche and deal-exposure mappings for the most relevant bonds. Collect new prediction-market data only if the question requires a named-storm probability path unavailable from these archived histories.
