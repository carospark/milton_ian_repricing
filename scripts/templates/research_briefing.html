The research roles

Four storms answer different parts of the question

Finding 01 · the trading constraint

Many eligible bonds, very few repeated observations near landfall

Several transactions in the same bond on one date do not create a price trajectory. Here, “repeated” means customer trading on at least two different dates in the eight-day interval from four days before through three days after landfall.

All strict eligible bond identifiersRepeated event-window trading
How to read these counts

The full inventory window is inclusive −45/+60: 106 calendar dates. Bond identifiers are counted separately for each storm; the same bond may appear in more than one storm. Counts must not be summed into a distinct market-wide bond total.

A strict identity match does not establish verified security class, active risk period, remaining collateral or sensitivity to the target storm. Customer buys mean the dealer sold; customer sells mean the dealer bought. Three prints before and after landfall can still come from very few dates.

Finding 02 · what expectations measure

Weather forecasts and market probabilities answer different questions

A forecast wind speed, a local wind-threshold probability, a US landfall contract and a bond’s probability of impairment are different outcomes. They cannot be substituted for one another.

Current advisory intensityForecast nearest 24 hours ahead

Knots of maximum sustained wind. The forecast uses an actual lead of 18–30 hours from the stated advisory issue time. Points include corrected archive versions. Dates are UTC; no exact landfall-time marker is asserted. Hover or tap a point for its observation.

NHC wind-probability grids

The GIS files combine active storms across basins. They give cumulative local threshold-exceedance probabilities over 0–120 hours at 34, 50 and 64 knots. Two fixed half-degree points per storm provide geographic context.

Nine UTC dates per storm, landfall −7/+1, every six hours. Beryl’s July 9 06/12/18 UTC cycles are not listed in the cached index. Missing points and threshold layers stay missing; initialization times are not verified publication times.

Finding 04 · descriptive repricing

The observed price changes depend on trading support and the sample

Explore the same-bond, same-customer-side comparisons below. More stringent trading-date requirements can substantially change both the count and composition of the sample.

Median price-point change relative to days −45/−8. An em dash means fewer than five paired bonds, so the statistic is suppressed; it does not mean zero change. Different windows may contain different bonds.

How the price comparisons are constructed

For each bond and customer side, first take the median of actual prints within an observed date. Then take the median of those daily medians within each window. Subtract the reference-window median from the comparison-window median. Dates receive equal weight, and nontrading dates are never filled.
